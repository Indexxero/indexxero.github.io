Three legs are usually tested in diligence: the schedule against invoices, against deferred revenue, and against cash. This pack covers the first leg only. The cash and deferred revenue legs are marked not covered in every table below, not filled in.

How the numbers were made

Matching
Customer id first; normalised name second (one match, Acme Corp to ACME Corporation, shown in the match table); anything else by hand. Unmatched on either side is a finding, not an error.
Invoice MRR
Recurring lines spread over their service period. Whole months split evenly, so an annual prepay is the same every month. Part months by days. A line with no service period lands in its invoice month and is flagged.
Credits
Netted into the month they belong to. Non recurring lines kept out of MRR and listed. Untyped lines flagged, not guessed.
Supported MRR
The schedule figure where the invoices back it within tolerance (1 percent or 5 units, whichever is larger); the lower of the two where they do not.
Tolerance
Set in the mapping file. Shown here at the default.
Reproducing
Same two files, same mapping, same command, same output. Hashes above identify the inputs.

What the files could not settle

Invoice lines with no type: 1. Lines with no service period: 14, thirteen for one account billed in arrears and the untyped line. Mapping warnings: 0. The kit does not read contracts, order forms, the general ledger or bank records. Where the schedule and the invoices disagree, it shows the disagreement and the lines behind it. Which figure is right is a question for the finance lead, and in a transaction, for the diligence team. Nothing here is an accounting opinion.

Running it

python3 pilot.py --schedule schedule.csv --invoices invoices.csv --map mapping.json --asof 2025-10 --out out

Runs on a laptop. Python and pandas. No network. Thirteen output files, including the full workbook and the per customer per month comparison. The files stay where they are run; nothing is uploaded anywhere.
