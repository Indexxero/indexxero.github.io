Three legs are usually tested in diligence: the schedule against invoices, against deferred revenue, and against cash. This pack covers the first two. The deferred revenue the invoices imply is rolled forward month by month below and compared with the balance the ledger carries. The cash leg is marked not covered in every table, not filled in.

Deferred revenue implied by the invoices

Billed is the invoice line in the month it was raised. Recognised is the share spread into each month of the service period. The difference, carried forward, is deferred revenue where billing runs ahead and unbilled revenue where recognition does. The two sides are reported apart, never netted. Here the only prepaid account is Copperline Freight, an annual invoice raised in November 2024, so the balance runs down by 2,000 a month to zero in October; the June unbilled 3,000 is the Cedar Loop credit memo in the month before its rebill.

The month end journal

journal.csv is the entry a QuickBooks Online user posts by hand today, one line per customer for the change in the deferred or unbilled balance, with the invoices behind it in the memo. Fourteen lines over the twelve months here: November 2024, debit subscription revenue 22,000, credit deferred revenue 22,000 (Copperline, invoice INV-1484); then every month, debit deferred revenue 2,000, credit subscription revenue 2,000, until October. A customer billed monthly for the month has no line. The accounts are named in plain words; it is a schedule to post from, not a posting and not an opinion on revenue recognition policy.

QuickBooks Online invoice exports carry no service period, so an annual invoice would otherwise land in one month and no deferred revenue would ever show. The kit gives such a line a period only when its amount is an exact whole multiple of that customer's schedule MRR, marks the line so, and leaves anything else in its month, flagged. A second fictional set in the QuickBooks shape, with a stated expected balance of 49,300 that the kit reproduces account by account, ships with the code.

How the numbers were made

Matching
Customer id first; normalised name second (one match, Acme Corp to ACME Corporation, shown in the match table); anything else by hand. Unmatched on either side is a finding, not an error.
Invoice MRR
Recurring lines spread over their service period. Whole months split evenly, so an annual prepay is the same every month. Part months by days. A line with no service period lands in its invoice month and is flagged.
Credits
Netted into the month they belong to. Non recurring lines kept out of MRR and listed. Untyped lines flagged, not guessed.
Supported MRR
The schedule figure where the invoices back it within tolerance (1 percent or 5 units, whichever is larger); the lower of the two where they do not.
Tolerance
Set in the mapping file. Shown here at the default.
Reproducing
Same two files, same mapping, same command, same output. Hashes above identify the inputs.

What the files could not settle

Invoice lines with no type: 1. Lines with no service period: 14, thirteen for one account billed in arrears and the untyped line. Mapping warnings: 0. The kit does not read contracts, order forms, bank records or general ledger detail; from the ledger it takes only the deferred revenue balance by month, given here as deferred_gl.csv. Where the schedule and the invoices disagree, it shows the disagreement and the lines behind it. Which figure is right is a question for the finance lead, and in a transaction, for the diligence team. Nothing here is an accounting opinion.

Running it

python3 pilot.py --schedule schedule.csv --invoices invoices.csv --map mapping.json --asof 2025-10 --out out --deferred-gl deferred_gl.csv

Runs on a laptop. Python and pandas. No network. Twenty one output files, including the full workbook, the bridge, the deferred roll forward, the journal, the per customer per month comparison and a receipt with the hash of every input and output, so the run can be verified by anyone holding the same files. The files stay where they are run; nothing is uploaded anywhere.
