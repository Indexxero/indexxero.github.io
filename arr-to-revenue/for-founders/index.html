ARR in the deck, revenue in the books: one month, worked

The ARR in a deck and the revenue in the books are different numbers, and both can be right. What matters is being able to say why they differ, customer by customer. Here is one month for Larkspur, a fictional SaaS company.

ARR on the schedule
868,800
ARR the invoices support
834,000
The gap
34,800

Where the gap comes from

  • Silent Partner Inc, 25,200 a year: on the schedule, with no invoice for the month.
  • Granite Peak Software, 9,600 a year: the schedule carries more than is billed.

Neither is a judgement on who is right. Each is a question with a document behind it: a contract, an amendment, an invoice in another system.

And the revenue for the month

The schedule carries 72,400 of recurring revenue for October; the books recognise 72,150, the invoices spread over the months they cover. The step by step walk between the two, account by account, is on the full worked bridge.

To forward to whoever keeps the books

arr-check compares the customer ARR schedule with the invoice lines, account by account, and walks ARR to recognised revenue and the deferred revenue balance, with a receipt anyone can recheck. It runs on the bookkeeper's own laptop or in a browser tab, and nothing is uploaded anywhere. The deferred revenue template is free: deferred_revenue_template.xlsx, and the worked month is at measure.indexxero.com/arr-to-revenue.

Larkspur does not exist; every figure comes from one run of the kit on its fictional example set. Not an accounting opinion. The code is public: github.com/Indexxero/arr-check.
