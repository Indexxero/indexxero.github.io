ARR to recognised revenue, a worked bridge

A reported ARR figure and the revenue in the books are different numbers, and both can be right. The work is to say exactly why they differ, account by account, and to show that every step traces to an invoice line. This page walks one month for a fictional SaaS company, Larkspur, using the files in the arr-check example folder (github.com/Indexxero/arr-check). Every figure below can be reproduced by running the kit on those files.

The two halves answer different questions. The top half asks how much of the reported ARR the invoices support. The bottom half asks what the books should show as revenue for the month. A schedule can be too high and too low at the same time, and netting the two would hide both.

Then to the balance sheet

Recognised revenue is the invoice spread over its service period. Billing ahead of service leaves deferred revenue; service ahead of billing leaves unbilled revenue. The roll forward for Larkspur:

At the end of October the invoices imply no deferred revenue, and the ledger carries 800. That 800 is the last step of the bridge. It has sat there every month of the year, which points to an old posting rather than this month's billing. The ledger gives one balance, not one per customer, so the question goes to whoever posts the journals. Deferred and unbilled are reported apart, never netted, for the same reason as above.

The checklist, before an ARR figure goes in a deck, a data room or a lender pack

  1. Does every customer on the schedule have an invoice for the month, and is every billed customer on the schedule?
  2. For customers on both, does the schedule amount equal the recurring billed amount? Where it does not, is the cause written down (price change, ramp, credit, rebill, timing)?
  3. Are credit memos and rebills netted against the month they correct, not the month they were raised?
  4. Are one off lines (setup, services, usage overage) kept out of ARR, and still accounted for in revenue? The kit lists them beside the bridge rather than inside it.
  5. Are duplicate invoice numbers caught before anything is summed?
  6. Does the schedule treat a churned customer as churned in the same month the billing stops?
  7. Is ARR stated twice, as reported and as supported, with the difference listed by account?
  8. Does recognised revenue for the month come from service periods, not invoice dates?
  9. Does the deferred revenue roll forward tie to the ledger balance, and is any difference explained?
  10. Can someone else rerun the whole thing from the same exports and get the same numbers?

The kit puts these questions to the exports on the firm's own laptop and writes the answers to plain files, with a receipt recording a hash of every input and output. It gives no accounting opinion. It makes the differences specific so the people who know the contracts can settle them.
