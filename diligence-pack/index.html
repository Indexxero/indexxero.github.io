Fictional company, synthetic data

A SaaS diligence pack: the schedules buyers and lenders ask for, ungated

When a SaaS company is bought, lends against its revenue or is audited, the same three schedules are asked for in writing: ARR by customer by month as a spreadsheet, not a screenshot; the movement from one period's ARR to the next, customer by customer; and the deferred revenue roll forward, tied to the balance the ledger carries. This workbook lays them out that way, blank to fill in, and filled for Larkspur, the fictional company in the arr-check example.

saas_diligence_pack.xlsx, blank: every total and check is a formula, ready to type into.

larkspur_diligence_pack.xlsx, filled from one run of the kit, every total tied to the run's files.

The five tabs

  • ARR by customer by month. One row per customer, one column per month, the ARR the schedule reports, with a total for each month.
  • ARR movement by customer. For each customer, opening ARR, new, reactivation, expansion, contraction, churn and closing ARR, on the schedule, on the invoices on their own, and on the figure the invoices support. A check column reads zero when a row adds up.
  • Deferred revenue. Month by month, billings against revenue recognised, the deferred and unbilled balances, and the ledger's balance beside them, with the difference.
  • Definitions. How each figure was counted, and where in the kit's code the rule is applied.
  • Read me. What each tab answers.
