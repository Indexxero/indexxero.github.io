Why it exists

Subscriptions that actually sync

The most common complaint about HubSpot's own Stripe app is subscription syncing, and its subscriptions need Enterprise custom objects. Recurbridge writes them to contacts and companies on any paid tier.

The fields people ask for

Status, plan, billing interval, amount, MRR, trial end, current period end, cancel at period end, cancelled date, Stripe customer id and last invoice status. Ready for lists, and for reports and workflows on Professional.

No duplicates, no silent gaps

Records match on the Stripe customer id first and email second. Every Stripe event is handled once, and a nightly check compares Stripe with HubSpot and repairs any gap.

Questions

Is it available now?

Not yet. It is being built with its first few founding customers, with a planned start in January 2027. No company uses it today, which is why the founding offer exists.

What will it read from Stripe?

Customers, subscriptions, subscription items, prices and invoice status, through a restricted key with read access to those objects only. It never sees card numbers.

What will it write to HubSpot?

Properties on contacts and companies for the fields above, and optionally one deal per subscription in a pipeline of the customer's choosing. It connects through HubSpot sign in with the fewest permissions needed and never deletes HubSpot records.

Which HubSpot tiers?

Starter, Professional and Enterprise. Reports and workflows on the synced fields need Professional, as with any HubSpot property. It does not require HubSpot payments.

Does it sync the other way?

No. Stripe stays the source of truth. One direction keeps the data trustworthy.

Historic subscriptions?

Yes. The first run fills in every active, trialing, past due and recently cancelled subscription.

What data is stored?

Only Stripe ids, HubSpot ids and the subscription fields above, encrypted in transit and at rest. Disconnecting the app in HubSpot or rolling the Stripe key ends access, and stored data is deleted within 30 days.
